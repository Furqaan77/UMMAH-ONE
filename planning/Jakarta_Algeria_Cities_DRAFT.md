## Jakarta, Indonesia

**Halal food and Islamic culture:** Halal food is easy to find almost everywhere, and Istiqlal Masjid, the largest masjid in Southeast Asia, stands at the heart of the capital.

**Calm:** Tranquility & Peace 2/10 · Healing 3/10

**Character:** Traditional 5/10 · Historic & Vintage 6/10 · Cultural 7/10 · Modern 9/10

**Energy:** Busy & Loud 10/10 · Too Much Tourism 4/10

Jakarta is big, loud and full of life: a sprawling capital of more than ten million people where glass towers, old Dutch warehouses and busy street markets sit side by side. Traffic is famous for the wrong reasons, so plan your days around one area at a time. The rewards are real: the grand Istiqlal Masjid, the colonial squares of Kota Tua, and a food scene built on Betawi dishes such as soto Betawi, nasi uduk and kerak telor.

Indonesia has the world's largest Muslim population, so halal food is the norm, the call to prayer carries across the city, and masjids are never far away. Costs are low by international standards, from street food to hotels. Malls are huge and air-conditioned, and many have prayer rooms. Mornings and late afternoons are the best times to be outside; the rainy season runs roughly from November to March.

### 20 Things to Do in Jakarta

1. Pray at Istiqlal Masjid, the largest masjid in Southeast Asia, and see its vast main hall.
2. Walk through the Tunnel of Friendship that links Istiqlal Masjid with Jakarta Cathedral across the road (to verify: open to visitors).
3. Ride the lift to the observation deck of Monas, the 132 m National Monument, and visit the independence museum at its base.
4. Rent a vintage bicycle and circle Fatahillah Square in Kota Tua, the old Dutch quarter.
5. Visit the Jakarta History Museum in the old City Hall on Fatahillah Square.
6. See Indonesian puppets at the Wayang Museum in Kota Tua.
7. Walk to Sunda Kelapa, the old harbour where wooden pinisi schooners still unload.
8. Take a boat from Marina Ancol to the Thousand Islands for snorkelling and beaches at Pulau Tidung or Pulau Pramuka.
9. Spend a day at Taman Mini Indonesia Indah, a park with houses and crafts from every province.
10. Discover Betawi culture, food and crafts at the Setu Babakan Betawi Cultural Village.
11. Visit the National Museum on Medan Merdeka for statues, ceramics and treasures from across the archipelago (to verify: opening times after the 2023 fire).
12. Join the Sunday morning Car Free Day around Bundaran HI and Jalan Thamrin.
13. Shop for fabric and modest fashion at Tanah Abang, one of Southeast Asia's largest textile markets.
14. Hunt for batik at Thamrin City or the batik floors of the big markets.
15. Wander Pasar Baru, one of the city's oldest shopping streets.
16. Spend an evening at Blok M for street food and the M Bloc creative space.
17. Relax by the sea at Ancol Dreamland on the north coast.
18. See orangutans and Komodo dragons at Ragunan Zoo in South Jakarta.
19. Browse the modern malls of Sudirman, such as Grand Indonesia and Plaza Indonesia, with prayer rooms inside.
20. Try kerak telor, the Betawi sticky-rice omelette, cooked over charcoal in Kota Tua.

### Food in Jakarta

1. Soto Betawi — beef soup in a rich coconut-milk and milk broth, Jakarta's signature soup.
2. Nasi uduk — rice cooked in coconut milk, served with fried chicken, egg and crisp shallots; the Betawi breakfast.
3. Kerak telor — sticky rice and egg cooked over charcoal, topped with toasted coconut and fried shallots.
4. Gado-gado — vegetables, tofu and egg in peanut sauce.
5. Ketoprak — rice cake, tofu and noodles in peanut sauce, a cheap street favourite.
6. Also try: Asinan Betawi (pickled vegetables); kue cubit (small sweet cakes); nasi goreng kambing (goat fried rice); es selendang mayang (iced rice-cake dessert).

**Where to eat:** Soto Betawi H. Ma'ruf, Jalan Cikini Raya (soto Betawi); Nasi Uduk Gang Buni, Jalan Mangga Besar (nasi uduk); Setu Babakan Betawi Cultural Village (Betawi dishes); Kota Tua street sellers (kerak telor). Check the dish, opening times and halal status before you go.

**Tip:** Most food is halal, but Glodok (Chinatown) and some Chinese restaurants serve pork; look for the Indonesian halal logo. Kerak telor sellers come out mostly at tourist spots and festivals.

## Algiers, Algeria

**Halal food and Islamic culture:** Halal food is the norm, and Islamic culture runs deep, from the 11th-century Great Masjid to Djamaa El Djazaïr, the largest masjid in Africa.

**Calm:** Tranquility & Peace 4/10 · Healing 4/10

**Character:** Traditional 7/10 · Historic & Vintage 8/10 · Cultural 8/10 · Modern 6/10

**Energy:** Busy & Loud 7/10 · Too Much Tourism 2/10

Algiers is "Alger la Blanche", a white city tumbling down green hills to the Mediterranean. At its heart is the Casbah, a UNESCO-listed maze of steep lanes, Ottoman palaces and old masjids; below it run broad French-era boulevards with arcades and grand balconies. Food here means couscous, rechta noodles in white sauce, chorba frik and syrupy qalb el louz from the Casbah bakeries.

Few foreign tourists come, so sights are quiet and people are curious and welcoming. Algeria is a Muslim-majority country and halal food is everywhere, though some hotels and restaurants serve alcohol. Costs are low. Spring (March to May) and autumn (September to November) are the best times to visit; summers are hot and humid.

### 20 Things to Do in Algiers

1. Explore the Casbah, the UNESCO-listed old city, with a local guide.
2. Visit Djamaa El Djazaïr, the largest masjid in Africa, and see the city from its minaret (to verify: minaret viewing deck open).
3. Pray at the Great Masjid (Djamaa El Kebir), one of the oldest masjids in Algeria.
4. See the Ketchaoua Masjid at the foot of the Casbah.
5. Step inside Dar Mustapha Pacha, an Ottoman palace covered in tiles.
6. Visit the Palace of the Raïs (Bastion 23), a group of Ottoman houses by the sea.
7. Take the cable car up to the Martyrs' Memorial (Maqam Echahid), lit green, white and red at night.
8. Learn the story of the independence war at the Museum of the Moudjahid beneath the memorial.
9. Walk through the Jardin d'Essai du Hamma, a large botanical garden from 1832.
10. See Algeria's prehistory and Roman mosaics at the Bardo Museum.
11. Visit the National Museum of Fine Arts above the Jardin d'Essai.
12. Admire Notre Dame d'Afrique, the 19th-century basilica on a cliff above the sea.
13. Walk the French-era arcades of Rue Didouche Mourad and Rue Larbi Ben M'hidi.
14. Photograph the Grande Poste, the white neo-Moorish post office.
15. Stroll the Sablettes seafront promenade at sunset.
16. Buy fish and taste grilled sardines at La Pêcherie, the old fishing port.
17. Pick up qalb el louz and makrout from a Casbah bakery.
18. Take a day trip to Tipasa to see Roman ruins by the sea (UNESCO).
19. Visit the Royal Mausoleum of Mauretania on the road to Tipasa.
20. Drink mint tea in a café on Place des Martyrs and watch the city go by.

### Food in Algiers

1. Rechta — thin noodles with chicken, chickpeas and white sauce.
2. Couscous — steamed semolina with vegetables and meat.
3. Dolma — stuffed vegetables in a light sauce.
4. Bourek — crisp savoury pastry.
5. Qalb el louz — syrup-soaked semolina sweet.
6. Also try: Chorba frik (cracked wheat soup); makrout.

**Where to eat:** Dar Lahlou (traditional Algerian cooking, menu check); La Pêcherie district (fish grills); Casbah bakeries (sweets). Check the dish, opening times and halal status before you go.

**Tip:** Rechta and white-sauce chicken dishes are more city-specific than generic couscous; confirm availability on the day.

## Oran, Algeria

**Halal food and Islamic culture:** Halal food is the norm, and the old Sidi El Houari quarter keeps the Pasha Masjid from 1797.

**Calm:** Tranquility & Peace 5/10 · Healing 5/10

**Character:** Traditional 6/10 · Historic & Vintage 7/10 · Cultural 7/10 · Modern 6/10

**Energy:** Busy & Loud 7/10 · Too Much Tourism 2/10

Oran is Algeria's second city: lively, sunny and proud, spread around a bay below Mount Murdjadjo. Spanish forts, Ottoman masjids and French boulevards all left their mark here. In the evening families fill the Front de Mer seafront; by day the city's own snack, karantika, is sold hot in bread from street stalls.

Halal food is everywhere, though some seafront restaurants serve alcohol, so ask. Costs are low and foreign tourists are few. The beaches west of the city are busy in summer, while spring and autumn are calmer and cooler.

11. Swim at the beaches of Aïn El Turck west of the city.

3. Oran-style seafood rice — a Mediterranean-influenced meal.

**Where to eat:** Medina Jdida street stalls (karantika); local bakeries (mouna). Check the dish, opening times and halal status before you go.

## Constantine, Algeria

**Halal food and Islamic culture:** Halal food is the norm, and the city is home to the Emir Abdelkader Masjid, one of the largest masjids in Africa, and a long tradition of Islamic learning.

**Calm:** Tranquility & Peace 5/10 · Healing 5/10

**Character:** Traditional 8/10 · Historic & Vintage 8/10 · Cultural 8/10 · Modern 4/10

**Energy:** Busy & Loud 6/10 · Too Much Tourism 1/10

Constantine is the "City of Bridges", built on a rock above the deep Rhumel gorge. Bridges and footbridges leap across the chasm, and the old town is full of Ottoman houses, old masjids and busy markets. The food is its own: chakhchoukha, trida and tlitli pasta dishes, and jawziya walnut nougat.

Constantine is one of Algeria's great centres of Islamic scholarship and Andalusian heritage, and it feels deeply traditional. Very few foreign tourists come. Halal food is the norm and costs are low. Winters are cold and wet; spring and autumn are the best seasons.

### Food in Constantine

1. Chakhchoukha constantinoise — shredded flatbread in a meat and chickpea sauce.
2. Trida — small square pasta in sauce.
3. Tlitli — small pasta grains with meat and chickpeas.
4. Jawziya — walnut nougat.
5. Tamina — toasted semolina with honey and butter.

**Where to eat:** Rahbet Essouf market and traditional kitchens (chakhchoukha and trida); local jawziya confectioners (nougat); Cirta-area restaurants (regional cooking by request). Check the dish, opening times and halal status before you go.
