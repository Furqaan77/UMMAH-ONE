# Shopping and Craft Guide — what to buy in each city and how to check it is real

Part of Ummah One's art and culture guide (the Ummah One team's "76-City Local Shopping & Craft Guide, 2026"). For each city: ideas of what to buy, and a note on where the items really come from. The list is not ranked. An item sold in a city is not always made there; some come from nearby villages, the region or the whole country. Items are to verify: shops, stock and prices change. Morocco is not in this guide (see What to Buy).

Tips before paying: ask who made the item and where; compare at least two sellers; check if it is handmade, hand-finished or factory-made. For gold, pearls, gemstones and carpets, ask for a dated receipt and a certificate. For food and cosmetics, check the ingredients, the maker, sealed packing and the expiry date. Check airline and customs rules before carrying food, liquids, plant or animal products, knives or antiques. Do not buy coral, wildlife products or old archaeological objects.

## Malaysia: 20 local crafts, souvenirs and specialties

What to buy in Malaysia (Kuala Lumpur, Penang, Langkawi and the rest of the country), from the Ummah One Malaysia keepsake guide:
- Malaysian Batik (Textiles & Clothing): Hand-drawn (batik lukis) or block-stamped (batik cap) fabrics featuring vibrant floral and geometric motifs; available as sarongs, scarves, shirts, and tote bags.
- Royal Selangor Pewterware: High-grade tin-alloy items tied to Malaysia’s tin-mining heritage, including coasters, photo frames, tea caddies, and miniature Petronas Twin Towers.
- Songket Fabric: Luxurious handwoven silk or cotton brocade intricately threaded with metallic gold or silver, traditionally worn for royal and ceremonial occasions.
- Peranakan / Nyonya Beadwork (Kasut Manek): Intricate, hand-stitched glass seed-bead slippers, pouches, and decorative accessories originating from Melaka and Penang.
- Peranakan Tiles & Porcelain: Colorful, pastel-toned encaustic floor tiles (great as coasters or framed wall art) and floral kamcheng ceramic jars.
- Sarawak Black & White Pepper: World-renowned, aromatic peppercorns grown in Borneo, sold in lightweight vacuum-sealed packs.
- Ipoh White Coffee: Roasted with palm-oil margarine for a rich, caramelized flavor; sold in convenient, lightweight instant sachet packs.
- BOH Cameron Highlands Tea: Loose-leaf black teas and flavored blends grown in Malaysia’s cool mountain estates.
- Sarawak Pua Kumbu Textiles: Traditional ceremonial ikat-dyed cotton cloths woven by the Iban people of Borneo, featuring bold tribal and nature motifs.
- Rattan & Pandan Woven Crafts: Hand-plaited baskets, bags, floor mats, and hats crafted by indigenous Orang Asli and Sabah/Sarawak artisans.
- Sabah Cultured Pearls: Freshwater and sea pearl necklaces, earrings, and bracelets sold at great value in Kota Kinabalu markets.
- Wau Bulan (Traditional Moon Kite): Miniature decorative versions of Kelantan’s iconic crescent-shaped, intricately patterned traditional kite.
- Kelantanese Silver Filigree Jewelry: Fine, handcrafted silver brooches (kerongsang), bangles, and pendants featuring traditional Malay floral patterns.
- Sarawak Kek Lapis (Layered Cake): Vibrant, geometric, multi-layered spiced butter cakes sold in neat, travel-friendly boxed loaves.
- Dodol & Kuih Kapit (Traditional Sweets): Chewy coconut-and-palm-sugar toffee (dodol, often flavored with durian or pandan) and crisp folded coconut wafer biscuits (love letters).
- Borneo Beadwork (Pinakol): Colorful geometric beaded necklaces, belts, and wristbands handmade by the Rungus community in Sabah.
- Traditional Woodcarvings (Ukiran Kayu): Hand-carved tropical hardwood wall panels, trays, and decorative boxes featuring Islamic geometric and floral designs.
- Beryl’s & Local Artisan Chocolates: Famous Malaysian-made chocolates, especially durian-filled chocolates and single-origin Malaysian cacao bars.
- Penang Nutmeg Balms & Oils: Traditional medicinal ointments, massage oils, and preserved nutmeg snacks produced on Penang Island.
- Congkak Board Game: A traditional wooden mancala-style counting game carved into a boat-shaped board, doubling as an authentic cultural decor piece.

Good to know: check airline and customs rules before buying food, liquids, plant products, pearls or wooden items. For handmade items, ask the seller about the maker, the materials and where it was made.
