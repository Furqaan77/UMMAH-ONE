# The University City Compass — 18 education cities, scores out of 10

Ummah One points students to 18 education cities. Each city is scored from 1 to 10 on the 10 priorities of university students (10 = outstanding, 8 = strong, 6 = adequate, 4 = limited, 2 = weak). There is no overall ranking, total or average: students should weigh the priorities that matter to them. Scores are indicative judgements, not measurements, as of November 2026. Source: The University City Compass report (QS World University Rankings 2026 and 2027, QS Arab Region Rankings 2026, U.S. News Best Global Universities 2026), scores doubled from 5 to 10 and re-checked against Ummah One's 10 criteria. Information only: no scholarships and no application help. To verify.

## The 10 priorities of university students

The goal: to graduate capable, thoughtful, and adaptable, ready not just for a job but for life, while lifting others up along the way.

- Quality education: Strong teaching, relevant and up-to-date courses, and respected degrees that equip students for a changing world.
- Affordability: Manageable tuition and living costs, plus scholarships and financial aid.
- Safety and convenience: Secure housing, reliable transport, accessible healthcare, and everyday services within easy reach.
- Personal growth: Building confidence, discipline, and resilience, along with healthy relationships and lasting friendships.
- Broader horizons: Meeting new people, exploring cultures, and exchanging ideas with open-minded peers who see the world differently.
- Experience and wisdom: Gaining maturity from challenges, mistakes, responsibility, and everyday life.
- Independence and flexibility: Managing money, solving problems, and thriving in unfamiliar situations.
- Career readiness: Hands-on exposure through projects, research, internships, and apprenticeships, while building a professional network with employers.
- Collaboration and community: Sharing expertise, working toward common goals, and combining strengths so everyone achieves more.
- Lifelong opportunity: Expanding possibilities with knowledge, skills, continuous learning, and meaningful contribution.

## Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia

Study in Kuala Lumpur. Universities: Universiti Malaya (UM). Southeast Asia's most complete student package: a world top-60 university, English-medium teaching, and one of the lowest costs of any ranked university city.

- Quality education: 10/10. UM is 58th in QS WUR 2026 and 56th in 2027. Malaysia's flagship, with top-100 subjects across engineering, computer science and business.
- Affordability: 10/10. Public tuition is a few thousand USD per year; comfortable student life on roughly USD 500–800 a month.
- Safety and convenience: 8/10. Generally safe with good metro links; routine big-city caution against petty theft.
- Personal growth: 8/10. Active campus clubs, leadership programmes and a lively student-city scene.
- Broader horizons: 10/10. Malay, Chinese and Indian cultures meet in one English-speaking, Muslim-majority metropolis.
- Experience and wisdom: 8/10. ASEAN's business hub gives real industry exposure alongside study.
- Independence and flexibility: 8/10. Easy independent living, friendly student visas, halal food everywhere.
- Career readiness: 10/10. Strong regional job market in finance, tech and energy; degrees well recognised across Asia.
- Collaboration and community: 10/10. Tens of thousands of international students; Malaysia is a top global study destination.
- Lifelong opportunity: 10/10. Post-study work options and alumni networks spanning ASEAN and the wider Muslim world.

## Istanbul, Türkiye

Study in Istanbul. Universities: Istanbul Technical University · Boğaziçi University · Istanbul University · Koç University · Sabancı University. Five world-ranked universities in a city that is itself an education: Europe and Asia in one skyline.

- Quality education: 10/10. ITU (298th), Boğaziçi (371), Koç (323) and Sabancı (404) all sit in the QS WUR 2026 top tier; ITU is 91st worldwide for Engineering & Technology.
- Affordability: 7/10. Public tuition is low, but inflation has raised living costs; private universities are costly without their generous scholarships. (Score changed from 6 to 7: Scholarships now count, and private universities offer generous ones.)
- Safety and convenience: 6/10. A vast, thrilling but demanding megacity: traffic, crowding and economic volatility require adjustment.
- Personal growth: 10/10. Unmatched cultural life: arts, debate, volunteering and student societies in a city of 16 million.
- Broader horizons: 10/10. A living crossroads of civilisations; few cities widen a student's worldview faster.
- Experience and wisdom: 9/10. Centuries of scholarship plus a large modern industry base to learn from. (Score changed from 8 to 9: A demanding megacity gives daily challenges that build maturity.)
- Independence and flexibility: 9/10. Navigating the megacity builds self-reliance quickly; some Turkish helps daily life. (Score changed from 8 to 9: Getting around the megacity builds self-reliance quickly.)
- Career readiness: 8/10. Türkiye's largest economy: deep internship pools in engineering, finance, media and trade.
- Collaboration and community: 8/10. Huge student communities and strong Erasmus and exchange links with Europe.
- Lifelong opportunity: 8/10. Powerful alumni networks across Türkiye, Europe and the Middle East.

## Riyadh, Saudi Arabia

Study in Riyadh. Universities: King Saud University (KSU) · Princess Nourah bint Abdulrahman University (PNU). The Kingdom's academic capital, riding the Vision 2030 wave of investment in education and research.

- Quality education: 8/10. KSU ranks 143rd in QS WUR 2026 and 4th in the QS Arab Region 2026; PNU is the world's largest women's university.

- Personal growth: 6/10. Conservative social rules are easing fast, but leisure options remain narrower than in Dubai or Doha.
- Broader horizons: 6/10. More local in character than the coastal Gulf hubs: deep Saudi culture, fewer foreign residents.
- Experience and wisdom: 6/10. A serious, structured study environment with growing research exposure.
- Independence and flexibility: 6/10. Campus life is organised and supportive, though less free-form than Western cities.
- Career readiness: 10/10. Vision 2030 megaprojects, Aramco and the banking sector create exceptional graduate demand.
- Collaboration and community: 6/10. Gender-segregated campuses shape a different, though close-knit, community experience.
- Lifelong opportunity: 8/10. Strong ladder into Gulf careers, government programmes and scholarships abroad.
